# Summary

The protagonist spent eleven days crossing the **Rust Marches** to **Corren's Stake**, a fortified outpost, where elder **Thessaly Drum-Walker** shared intelligence about temporal disturbances at **Redsong Gulch**. They partnered with **Vesker**, a young scavenger with involuntary temporal foresight — **natural Drift** managed with a tuning fork. Vesker probed the gulch's rhythm and identified it as a **counterbeat**: the Marches' temporal signature played backward with machine-like precision. The probe created a two-way connection, making the gulch aware of them. Vesker declared it **a door**.

A dead traveler's **journal** mapped Redsong's **southern chamber** as a temporal inversion point, its inverse cadence a **signal** meant to **open time itself**. A self-destroying message in temporally-charged ink warned against harmonizing with the signal, stating what waited beyond had existed since the **First Lament** and was "not patient — simply **SLOW**." Vesker identified these as communications of the **Lament Seekers** and **the Metronome** — a sentient entity whose name, spoken aloud, caused the southern pulse to surge then fall silent, confirming awareness.

Overnight, the rhythm tested them — the lantern pulsed near-alignment, red grass traced alien letters, and the tempo accelerated as if excited. **Vesker's Drift worsened**: visible ghost-images now trailed their movements, and they flickered in sleep. At dawn they broke camp and walked south through bamboo-like stalks coated in reddish resin until the grass parted, revealing **Redsong Gulch** — a two-hundred-foot chasm whose depths rippled with layered translucent moments like stacked glass, impossibly precise mechanical **ticking** rising from below.

At the rim, they discovered a **flickering figure**. When the protagonist asked Vesker to read the stranger's intentions, Vesker **recoiled in panic** — the figure's timeline was *shredded*, existing in six moments simultaneously with no futures aligning. Vesker identified it as **heavy Drift far beyond their own**, the result of enough Cadence practice to half-eject someone from linear time.

The figure — **Endric Vael** — shifted upright with intervening motion simply absent. Gaunt, sharp-featured, wearing clothing that refused to belong to any single era, his edges flickered constantly and his words arrived a half-breath before his lips formed them. He identified them as **Thessaly's errand runners** and claimed to have sat at the rim for an indeterminate period — three days or five hours. A former member of the **Order of the Unblinking**, expelled for "inconvenient methods," Vael had been studying the gulch for longer than he could reliably measure. He immediately recognized Vesker's condition as **natural Drift** rather than practiced Cadence, reacting with brief, genuine pity. Grass aged and desiccated around his boots as he stepped closer. He posed a blunt question: whether they came to help him, hinder him, or simply die interestingly — noting the gulch accommodated all three.

**Key details:** The Metronome is sentient, connected to the Lament Seekers, communicating via self-destructing messages. The journal maps Redsong's southern chamber as the inversion point; harmonizing risks opening the door from both sides. Vesker's Drift is accelerating with visible ghost-images. The gulch's ticking is mechanical. **Endric Vael** — ex-Order of the Unblinking, heavily time-displaced — awaits them at the rim, his timeline unreadable even to Vesker's foresight.
