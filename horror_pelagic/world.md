# The Thinning: Briar Sound and the Geometry Beneath

## Creation Myth — Or What Passes For One

The indigenous Passamaquoddy people who once fished these waters had a name for this stretch of Maine coastline that translates roughly to "the place where the world forgets itself." They did not build permanent settlements here. Their oral traditions speak of a time before time when the sea floor cracked open and something that was not water rose up — something that looked like water, moved like water, but dreamed. They called it *Kchi-Sipihkuk*, the Great Wound, and they said the land around it grew confused, unable to remember what shape it was supposed to be.

European settlers, naturally, ignored all of this.

## The Town of Briar Sound

Briar Sound, Maine (pop. 2,307 — though the number on the welcome sign hasn't been updated since 1998, and no one can agree on the current count) sits at the convergence of Kettle Bay and the Acheron River estuary. It is the kind of town where people leave their doors unlocked, where the diner coffee is reliable, and where, if you drive twenty minutes in any direction, your radio will find nothing but static and a low, rhythmic pulsing that sounds almost like breathing.

The town exists because of the Briar Sound Cannery, which operated from 1923 to 1987, processing fish that came from waters so abundant they seemed almost *eager* to be caught. The cannery closed after what locals call "the Sour Season" — a three-month period when every fish pulled from Kettle Bay was found to contain, instead of normal organs, a series of nested geometric structures made of cartilage and black fluid. State health inspectors called it a parasite. Marine biologists from Orono called it "unprecedented." Old Hattie Marsh, who'd cleaned fish at the cannery for forty years, called it "the insides remembering what they used to be."

## The Power System — Geometric Resonance

Reality in and around Briar Sound operates on a principle that no human science has vocabulary for, though several have tried. The closest approximation: the physical world is a pattern, a frequency, and that frequency can be disrupted, harmonized with, or overwritten. Certain geometries — angles, curves, spatial relationships — act as tuning forks for the underlying substrate of reality. The Passamaquoddy understood this intuitively. The thing beneath Kettle Bay understands it absolutely.

Those who study the geometries (found carved in coastal rocks, sketched in the margins of old cannery ledgers, appearing spontaneously in frost patterns on winter windows) can learn to perceive the thinness of the world. Some can push through it. The cost is always cognitive — memories rearrange, personality fragments, the sense of self becomes negotiable. The geometries are not magic. They are closer to a language that the universe was not meant to be spoken to in.

## The Five Regions of Briar Sound

**1. The Town Proper** — Main Street, the defunct cannery, Maisie's Diner, the library, the harbor. Feels almost aggressively normal, like a postcard insisting everything is fine. Buildings lean at angles that are *almost* right.

**2. Kettle Bay & The Sounding Rocks** — The bay itself, deep and cold and oddly still even in storms. The Sounding Rocks are a formation of basalt columns on the northern shore that produce audible tones when the tide changes. The tones have been recorded and analyzed: they form non-repeating mathematical sequences.

**3. The Acheron Estuary & Salt Marshes** — Miles of tidal flats, cord grass, and fog so thick it has texture. People get lost here for hours and come back different — not dramatically, but in small ways. A preference for a food they used to hate. A birthmark slightly relocated. The marshes are tidal, and what the tide reveals is not always what the tide took.

**4. Harrow Hill & The Old Parish** — The hill overlooking town, crowned by St. Elmo's Parish, abandoned since 1971 when Father Calloway walked into the bay during a sermon and was never recovered. The church basement contains extensive records — and something else. The hill's geological composition does not match any surrounding formation. Core samples taken in 1984 revealed a uniform, spiraling internal structure descending further than the drill could reach.

**5. The Cannery District & Worker Housing** — Derelict now, but not empty. Squatters and drifters sometimes shelter here, though they rarely stay long. The walls of the cannery are covered in graffiti that, on close inspection, predates the building itself — etched into the brick from the *inside*. The worker housing units are arranged in a pattern that, when viewed from above, forms a shape that hurts to look at on satellite imagery. Google Maps blurs this area.

## Major Historical Events

- **1691**: First European settlement attempt. All colonists found alive but catatonic, arranged in a circle on the beach, facing inward toward a point where the sand had been fused to glass.
- **1923**: Briar Sound Cannery founded by Aldous Harrow. Town prospers unnaturally.
- **1971**: Father Calloway's disappearance. His last sermon, partially transcribed, describes "the angle at which God's attention bends away from us."
- **1987**: The Sour Season. Cannery closes. Seventeen workers disappear over the following year.
- **2019**: Marine survey vessel *Pelican* maps the bay floor and discovers a trench of impossible depth. Data is classified. Survey crew members have since been committed to various psychiatric facilities.
- **Present Day (October 2024)**: Tides have become irregular. Dogs in town howl in unison at 3:17 AM every night. The geometry is asserting itself.

## Current Tensions

The town is split between those who want to sell the waterfront to a development company (Pelagic Properties, whose corporate registration leads to a chain of shell companies terminating in a P.O. box that does not exist) and longtime residents who sense, without articulating, that disturbing the bay would be catastrophic. The town selectman has gone missing. The new harbor master measures things constantly — angles, distances, the spaces between things — and will not say why. And three days ago, a woman washed ashore on the Sounding Rocks, alive, speaking a language that recordings cannot capture, wearing clothes that won't be manufactured for another six years.
